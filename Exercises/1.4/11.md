Suppose $A$ and $B$ are sets. Is it necessarily true that $(A\setminus B)\cup B=A$? If not, is one of these sets necessarily a subset of the other? Is $(A\setminus B)\cup B$ always equal to either $A\setminus B$ or $A\cup B$?

$x \in(A\setminus B)\cup B$ is equivalent to $(x \in A\land x\not\in B)\lor x \in B$, which is equivalent to $x \in A\lor x \in B$, which is equivalent to $x \in A\cup B$. So $(A\setminus B)\cup B=A\cup B\neq A$. 

$A\subseteq A\cup B=(A\setminus B)\cup B$.

| Question                                                     | Answer                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Is it necessarily equal to $A$?                           | No.                                            |
| 2. Is one necessarily a subset of the other?                 | Yes, $A\subseteq A\cup B=(A\setminus B)\cup B$ |
| 3. Is it always equal to either $A\setminus B$ or $A\cup B$? | Yes, it is always equal to $A\cup B$.          |
