# Quantifiers
Sometimes we want to say something about how many values of $x$ make $P(x)$ come out true. In particular, we often want to say either that $P(x)$ is true for every value of $x$ or that it is true for at least one value of $x$. We therefore introduce 2 more symbols, called quantifiers, to help us express these ideas.

To say that $P(x)$ is true for every value of $x$ in the universe of discourse $U$, we will write $\forall xP(x)$. This is read "For all $x$, $P(x)$." The symbol $\forall$ is called the universal quantifier, because the statement $\forall xP(x)$ say that $P(x)$ is universally true. $P(x)$ is true for every value of $x$ in the universe means that the truth set of $P(x)$ will be the whole universe $U$. Thus, you could think of the statement $\forall xP(x)$ saying that the truth set of $P(x)$ is equal to $U$.

We write $\exists xP(x)$ to say that there is at least one value of $x$ in the universe for which $P(x)$ is true. This is read "There exists an $x$ such that $P(x)$." The backward $E$ comes from the word exists and is called the existential quantifier. To say that $P(x)$ is true for at least one value of $x$ means that there is at least one element in the truth set of $P(x)$, or in other words, the truth set is not equal to $\emptyset$.

In general, even if $x$ is free variable in some statement $P(x)$, it is a bound variable in some statement $\forall xP(x)$ and $\exists xP(x)$. For this reason, we say that the quantifiers **bind** a variable.

Example 2.1.2. Analyse the logcial forms of the following statements:
1.Someone didn't do the homwork.
2.Everything in that store is either overpriced or poorly made.
3.Nobody's perfect.
4.Susan likes everyone who dislikes Joe.
5.$A\subseteq B$.
6.$A\cap B\subseteq B\setminus C$.

1.$\exists x\neg H(x)$, where $H(x)$ stands for "$x$ did the homework."
2.$\forall x[S(x)\to(O(x)\lor P(x))]$, where $S(x)$ stands for "$x$ is in that store." $O(x)$ stands for "$x$ is overpriced." and $P(x)$ stands for "$x$ is poorly made."
3.$\neg \exists P(x)$, where $P(x)$ stands for "$x$ is perfect."
4.$\forall x(\neg L(x,j)\to L(s,x))$, where $L(x,y)$ stand for "$x$ likes $y$."
5.$\forall x(x \in A\to x \in B)$.
6.$\forall x[(x \in A\land x \in B)\to(x \in B\land x\not\in C)]$.

1.Analyze the logical forms of the following statements.

(b) Nobody in the calculus class is smarter then everybody in the discrete math class.
(c) Everyone likes Mary, except Mary herself.
(d) Jane saw a police officer, and Roger saw one too.
(e) Jane saw a police officer, and Roger saw him too.

(a) 

$\forall x(\text{If }x\text{ has forgiven at least one person, }x\text{ is a saint})$,
$\forall x(\exists y(x\text{ has forgiven y})\to x\text{ is a saint})$
$\forall x(\exists yF(x,y)\to S(x))$
, where $F(x,y)$ stands for "$x$ has forgiven $y$," $S(x)$ stands for "$x$ is a saint."

(b) 

$\neg \exists x(x\text{ is in the calculus class}\land x\text{ is smarter then everybody in the discrete math class})$
$\neg \exists x(x\text{ is in the calculus class}\land \forall y (\text{If y is in the discrete math class, }x\text{ is smarter then }y))$
$\neg \exists x(C(x)\land \forall y(D(y)\to S(x,y)))$,
where $C(x)$ stands for "$x$ is in the calculus class," $D(y)$ stands for "$y$ is in the discrete math class." $S(x,y)$ stands for "$x$ is smarter then $y$."

(c)

$\forall x(x\neq \text{Mary}\to x\text{ likes Mary})$
$\forall(x\neq m\to L(x,m))$.
, where $m$ stands for Mary, $L(x,y)$ stands for "$x$ likes $y$."
(d)

$\exists x(\text{x is a police officer}\land \text{Jane saw } x)\land \exists y(y\text{ is police officer}\land \text{Roger saw }y)$,
$\exists x(P(x)\land S(j,x))\land \exists y(P(y)\land S(r,y))$,
, where $j$ stands for Jane, $r$ stands for Roger, $P(x)$ stands for "$x$ is a police officer," $S(x,y)$ stands for "$x$ saw $y$."

$\exists x(x\text{ is a police officer}\land \text{Jane saw }x\land \text{Roger saw }x)$,
$\exists x(P(x)\land S(j,x)\land S(r,x))$.

, where $F(x)$ stands for "$x$ failed the test," $A(x)$ stands for "$x$ got an A," $T(x,y)$ stands for "$x$ tutor $y$," $D(y)$ stands for "$y$ got a D."

no free variables
(c)
$\forall y(y^3-3y<3\to y<10)$.
no free variables
