24.Let $R=\{ (m,n)\in \mathbb{N}\times \mathbb{N}\mid{}\lvert m-n \rvert\leq 1 \}$, which is a relation on $\mathbb{N}$. Note that $R\subseteq \mathbb{Z}\times \mathbb{Z}$, so $R$ is also a relation on $\mathbb{Z}$. This exercise will illustrate why, in part 1 of Definition 4.3.2, we defined the phrase "$R$ is reflexive on $A$," rather than simply "$R$ is reflexive."

(a) Is $R$ reflexive on $\mathbb{N}$?

(b) Is $R$ reflexive on $\mathbb{Z}$?

(a) Let $n\in \mathbb{N}$ be arbitrary. $\lvert n-n \rvert=0\leq 1$. $(n,n)\in R$. Thus, $R$ is reflexive on $\mathbb{N}$.

(b) $R$ is not reflexive on $\mathbb{Z}$. Counterexample: Let $n=-1\in \mathbb{Z}$, $(n,n)\not\in R$ because $n\not\in \mathbb{N}$.
