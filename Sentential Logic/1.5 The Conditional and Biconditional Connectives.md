# The Conditional and Biconditional Connectives
$P\to Q$ is to represent the statement "If $P$ then $Q$." This statement is sometimes called a conditional statemnt, with $P$ as its antecedent and $Q$ as its consequence.


Conditional laws
$P\to Q$ is equivalent to $\neg P\lor Q$.
$P\to Q$ is equivalent to $\neg(P\land \neg Q)$.

$Q\to P$ is called the **converse** of $P\to Q$.

The **contrapositive** of  $P\to Q$ is the formula $\neg Q\to \neg P$.

Contrapositive law
$P\to Q$ is equivalent to $\neg Q\to \neg P$.

express $P\to Q$:
$P$ implies $Q$.
$Q$, if $P$.
$P$ only if $Q$.
$P$ is a sufficient condition for $Q$.
$Q$ is a necessary condition for $P$.

A statement of the form $P\leftrightarrow Q$ is called a **biconditional** statement, because it represents two conditional statement. 

$P\leftrightarrow Q$ is often written "$P$ **iff** $Q$."

Another statement that means $P\leftrightarrow Q$ is "$P$ is a **necessary and sufficient condition** for $Q$."

(b) Having both a fever and a headache is a sufficient condition for George to go to the doctor.
(c) Both having a fever and having a headache are sufficient conditions for George to go to the doctor.
(d) If $x\neq 2$, then a necessary condition for $x$ to be prime is that $x$ be odd.
