# Deductive Reasoning and Logical Connectives

We have arrived at a [[conclusion]] from the assumption that some other statements, called [[Definitions/premise]], are true.

We will say that an [[argument]] is [[valid]] if the premises cannot all be true without the conclusion being true as well.
我们称一个论证是有效的，如果在前提全部为真的情况下，结论不可能为假。

One argument  is invalid when its conclusion could be false even if both premises are true.

It will be useful to use symbols called [[connective symbol]]s to stand for some of the words used to combine statements.

| Symbol  | Meaning |
| ------- | ------- |
| $\lor$  | or      |
| $\land$ | and     |
| $\neg$  | not     |


The statement $P\lor Q$ is sometimes called the disjunction of $P$ and $Q$, $P\land Q$ is called the conjunction of $P$ and $Q$, and $\neg P$ is called the negation of P.

The symbol $\land$ and $\lor$ can only be used between two statements, to form their conjunction or disjunction, and the symbol $\neg$ can only be used before a statement.

"Grammatical" expressions, are sometimes called **well-formed formulas** or just **formulas**.

$3\leq \pi$ is written as $(3 < \pi)\lor(3=\pi)$. $3\leq \pi<4$ is written as $[(3 < \pi)\lor(3=\pi)]\land(\pi<4)$

5.Which of the following expressions are well-formed formulas?
(a) $\neg(\neg P\lor \neg \neg R)$.
(b) $\neg(P, Q, \land R)$.
(c) $P\land \neg P$

6.Let P stand for the statement “I will buy the pants” and S for the statement “I will buy the shirt.” What English sentences are represented by the following formulas?
(a) $\neg(P\land \neg S)$.
(b) $\neg P\land \neg S$.
(c) $\neg P\lor \neg S$

(a) I won’t buy the pants without the shirt.
(b) I won’t buy the pants and I won’t buy the shirt.
(c) Either I won’t buy the pants or I won’t buy the shirt.

7.Let S stand for the statement “Steve is happy” and G for “George is happy.” What English sentences are represented by the following formulas?
(a) $(S\lor G)\land(\neg S\lor \neg G)$
(b) $[S\lor(G\land \neg S)]\lor \neg G$
(c) $S\lor[G\land(\neg S\lor \neg G)]$

(a) Exactly one of Steve and George is happy.
(b) Either Steve is happy, or George is not happy, or George is happy while Steve is not./This is a tautology.
(c) Steve is happy, or George is happy.

8.Let $T$ stand for the statement “Taxes will go up” and $D$ for “The
deficit will go up.” What English sentences are represented by the
following formulas?
(a) $T\lor D$.
(b) $\neg(T\land D)\land \neg(\neg T\land \neg D)$
(c) $(T\land \neg D)\lor(D\land \neg T)$

(a) Either taxes or the deficit will go up.
(b) Either taxes will go up or the deficit will go up, but not both.
(c) Either taxes will go up and the deficit will not, or the deficit will go up and taxes will not.
